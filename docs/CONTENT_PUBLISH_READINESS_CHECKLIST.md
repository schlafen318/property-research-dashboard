# Content Publish-Readiness Checklist

Use this checklist for every guide, comparison, destination article, and calculator explainer before publishing. A page is publish-ready only when every required item is complete or explicitly marked not applicable with a reason.

## 1. Reader intent and scope

- [ ] The title, introduction, body, FAQ, and metadata answer the same search intent.
- [ ] The article states who it is for and the decision it helps them make.
- [ ] The most important constraint appears before rankings, listings, or calls to action.
- [ ] Comparisons stay within the stated scope; contextual alternatives are clearly labelled.
- [ ] Every promise in the title and introduction is answered in the body.
- [ ] Generic framework copy has been replaced with country-, market-, or task-specific guidance.

## 2. Accuracy and evidence

- [ ] Legal, tax, immigration, healthcare, regulatory, and administrative claims cite current primary sources.
- [ ] Material claims distinguish national rules from municipal rules, building bylaws, lender policy, or professional judgment.
- [ ] Price, yield, cost, return, and market-size figures include a source, observation date, geography, asset definition, and currency basis.
- [ ] Estimates are labelled as estimates and show what is included or excluded.
- [ ] Unsupported precision has been removed.
- [ ] Ownership, residency, financing, tax residence, and healthcare eligibility are not presented as interchangeable.
- [ ] A qualified local professional can identify which claims require transaction-specific verification.

## 3. Freshness

- [ ] Every linked source was checked during the current review.
- [ ] The article reflects rules already in force on the displayed update date.
- [ ] Announced future changes are labelled with their effective date and current status.
- [ ] Active policy reviews are described as reviews, not enacted rules.
- [ ] The visible update date and structured-data date match the actual review date.
- [ ] A recheck trigger is recorded for time-sensitive rules or data.

## 4. Decision usefulness

- [ ] The article explains what would make the reader proceed, pause, or reject an option.
- [ ] Costs cover acquisition, ownership, operation, risk reserves, and exit—not only asking price.
- [ ] Financing constraints are addressed before affordability conclusions.
- [ ] The article covers the practical risks most likely to change the decision.
- [ ] Destination comparisons use criteria relevant to the article's stated purpose.
- [ ] The article identifies who the option suits and who should consider an alternative.
- [ ] The next-step checklist follows the order in which the reader should investigate.

## 5. Editorial quality

- [ ] The opening gives a direct answer without keyword-stuffed phrasing.
- [ ] Headings are descriptive and allow the article to be scanned quickly.
- [ ] Repetition, duplicated summaries, redundant cards, and unnecessary metadata have been removed.
- [ ] Caveats are specific and do not substitute for evidence.
- [ ] Claims are written in plain language and define necessary specialist terms.
- [ ] The article has a named author or research team and a visible update date.
- [ ] The tone is impartial; commercial calls to action do not interrupt essential risk information.

## 6. SEO and structured data

- [ ] The title and description are accurate, useful, and within their intended display length.
- [ ] One clear H1 matches the page's primary intent.
- [ ] The canonical URL is correct.
- [ ] Article schema includes headline, description, canonical URL, author, publisher, date published, and date modified.
- [ ] FAQ schema exactly matches the visible FAQ.
- [ ] Internal links are relevant, non-duplicative, and use descriptive anchors.
- [ ] In destination-led narrative, the first substantive mention of each featured destination links to its destination dossier and uses restrained semibold emphasis; later mentions in the same section remain plain text.
- [ ] External citations link directly to the authoritative supporting page.

## 7. Visual and technical QA

- [ ] The generated artifact was rebuilt from its source after editing.
- [ ] Desktop and mobile layouts have no horizontal overflow, clipped text, or inaccessible controls.
- [ ] Tables remain understandable on a narrow screen.
- [ ] Heading order and landmarks are logical.
- [ ] Links work and external sources open at the intended page.
- [ ] The page loads without browser-console errors.
- [ ] Relevant automated tests pass.

## Destination dossier standard

Apply this gate to every premium destination dossier in addition to the general checks above.

- [ ] The verdict appears before scores, listings, methodology, or promotional actions and states who should proceed, pause, or look elsewhere.
- [ ] Five editorial lenses cover all ten Atlas dimensions exactly once: lifestyle magnetism + retirement fit; global access + foreigner fit; ownership clarity + regulatory safety; rental profit + capital upside; value entry + exit liquidity.
- [ ] The narrative explains buyer consequences and does not repeat the score rationales.
- [ ] The page contains exactly one 10-row score table, and its weighted result is derived from the destination dataset rather than overridden in the article.
- [ ] Three to five representative listing observations show distinct buyer cases, use local currency first, record area and price per square metre, include a dated USD basis, link the source, state capture date and confidence, and carry an asking-price disclaimer.
- [ ] The visible benchmark, listing evidence, value-entry assessment, and confidence statement do not materially contradict one another.
- [ ] One compact micro-location table distinguishes daily-life pattern and primary diligence; it is not duplicated as cards.
- [ ] Six to eight buyer actions appear in investigation order.
- [ ] Exactly three destination-specific editorial images appear once each: one hero and two images distributed through relevant narrative sections. Do not use a montage.
- [ ] The rail contains no more than seven anchors, Atlas copy does not hard-code a destination count, and references are the final article section.
- [ ] Editorial prose is 1,800–2,400 words excluding tables and references; country-wide material is summarized and handed off to the country guide.
- [ ] Desktop and 390 × 844 mobile review confirm readable body text, contained table scrolling, useful image crops, no page overflow, and no browser-console errors.

## 8. Final publication gate

- [ ] A reviewer can identify the main recommendation, strongest caveat, and next step in under one minute.
- [ ] No high-stakes claim relies solely on internal scoring or unsourced prose.
- [ ] No visible statement contradicts the cited source or another part of the page.
- [ ] The article would still be useful if all promotional calls to action were removed.
- [ ] The final generated page—not only the source configuration—has been reviewed.
